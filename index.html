How I work

The same four steps on every project, whatever the tool.

  1. Pin down the decision

    Agree on what question the analysis must answer and what result would change someone’s mind.

A/B Test Analysis

Should the company launch its new landing page? A three-week experiment with 290,584 users, tested three ways.

Experiment analysis
Tools

Data
290,584 users, Jan 2017
Outcome

Summary

✓

Recommendation: keep the current page

The new page converted at 11.88% against 12.04% for the old one. A gap this small appears by chance most of the time, and the 95% confidence interval for the difference (−0.39 to +0.08 percentage points) rules out any meaningful lift. Launching would cost design and engineering time with no measurable return.

290,584unique users after cleaning
12.04%conversion, old page
11.88%conversion, new page
p = 0.905one-sided test for a lift

The question

Does showing users the new landing page increase the share who convert? I framed it as a one-sided test, because the business only cares about launching if the new page is better.

H₀: pnew − pold ≤ 0    H₁: pnew − pold > 0    α = 0.05

The data

Each row is a user visit with a timestamp, the group they were assigned to, the page they saw and whether they converted. The experiment ran from 2 to 24 January 2017.

  • Removed rows where the assigned group and the page shown didn’t match, since those users’ results can’t be trusted.
  • Dropped duplicate users so each person counts once.
  • Ended with 145,274 users in control and 145,310 in treatment, a near-even split that suggests randomisation worked.

Method

I tested the result three independent ways so the conclusion wouldn’t hinge on one technique.

  1. SimulationSimulated 10,000 experiments where both pages share the pooled conversion rate (11.96%) and compared the observed gap with that distribution.
  2. Two-proportion z-testz = −1.31, one-sided p = 0.905.
  3. Logistic regressionPage as the predictor, giving an effect size as an odds ratio.

All three methods agree. The regression’s two-sided p-value of 0.19 matches the z-test’s two-sided equivalent, and an odds ratio of 0.985 means users on the new page were about 1.5% less likely to convert, though that dip is also within noise.

Next steps

  • Size the next test up front. With about 145,000 users per group, this design could reliably detect a change of roughly 0.34 points (80% power). Smaller effects need more traffic or a longer run.
  • Run longer. Three weeks may not separate a real effect from change aversion or novelty among returning visitors.
  • Segment. Break results down by country and device to check whether the new page helps one audience while hurting another.

The takeaway for the business: the redesign isn’t worth launching as it stands. Spend the effort on a version with a bigger expected impact, and plan the test size before it starts.

Which artists, genres and customers drive the store’s revenue, and where should marketing focus?

Overview

An online music store wanted to understand where its sales come from so it could put marketing money behind the catalogue and customers that matter most. I translated that into three questions:

  • Which genres and artists bring in the most revenue?
  • Which customers and countries spend the most?
  • Which KPIs should the team track to see if marketing is working?

The data

A relational database covering the store’s catalogue and sales. Revenue lives at the invoice-line level, so every question means joining sales back to the catalogue or to customers.

Entity relationship diagram
Simplified to the tables used in the analysis. Highlighted tables and columns form the path from a sale to its genre.
PKPrimary key FKForeign key One Many Used in the genre revenue query

Approach

  1. Map the schemaTraced how each table links so every revenue figure could be tied to the right artist, genre or customer.
  2. Calculate revenueDefined revenue as unit price × quantity at the invoice-line level, then aggregated it up to each question’s level.
  3. Rank and compareRanked artists, genres, customers and countries by revenue to show where sales concentrate.
  4. Track KPIs in ExcelMoved the query results into Excel to chart them and set up KPIs the team can monitor.

Query example

Revenue by genre, following the highlighted path in the schema. The same pattern, with different joins, answers the artist and customer questions.

SELECT g.Name AS genre,
       ROUND(SUM(il.UnitPrice * il.Quantity), 2) AS revenue
FROM InvoiceLine il
JOIN Track t ON t.TrackId = il.TrackId
JOIN Genre g ON g.GenreId = t.GenreId
GROUP BY g.Name
ORDER BY revenue DESC
LIMIT 5;  -- top 5 genres

What I delivered

  • Ranked tables of the top-earning genres, artists, customers and countries.
  • Excel charts that make the concentration of revenue easy to see at a glance.
  • A short KPI set for tracking whether marketing changes move sales.

Overview

Travellers, airlines and airports all lose time and money to delays. The aim was a single dashboard where anyone could see how the US domestic network performed in 2015 and dig into specific airlines, airports or months without writing a query.

The data

The 2015 US Department of Transportation flights dataset: roughly 5.8 million domestic flights with scheduled and actual times, cancellations, and minutes of delay recorded by cause. Supporting tables map airline and airport codes to names and locations.

    Overview

    Exploratory analysis is the work that comes before any model or dashboard: understanding what the data contains, fixing what’s broken, and finding which patterns are real. This project covers that process end to end and answers a set of targeted questions with statistics and charts.

    Workflow

    Analysis pipeline
    Each step produces something concrete. Exploring often uncovers new issues, which sends the data back for more cleaning.
    1. LoadRead the raw data into Pandas and get a first look at its shape and columns.
    2. AssessCheck data types, missing values, duplicates and impossible values.
    3. CleanFix types, handle gaps, drop duplicates and columns that don’t serve the questions.
    4. ExploreAnswer one question at a time, from single-variable distributions to relationships between variables.
    5. ConcludeState findings with the evidence behind them and the limits of an observational dataset.

    Data quality checks

    CheckWhy it matters
    Missing valuesGaps can silently shrink or bias the sample behind a chart.
    DuplicatesRepeated rows inflate counts and averages.
    Data typesDates stored as text or numbers stored as strings break calculations.
    Impossible valuesZeros or negatives where they can’t occur point to entry errors.

    What I delivered

    • A cleaned, analysis-ready dataset with every cleaning step documented.
    • A written report answering each question with a clear chart and summary statistics.
    • An honest limitations section: correlation in observational data isn’t causation.
